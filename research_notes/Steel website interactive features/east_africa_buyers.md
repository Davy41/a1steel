# East African Steel / Building-Materials Buyers and Websites (Rwanda focus, 2025-2026)

Research date: 2026-11-23. Note: about 19 tool calls. Several company sites could not be read (see Gaps). Where a fetch result looked unreliable it was re-checked; one SteelRwa result was found to be invented by the fetch tool and has been **discarded** (see Gaps).

## 1. What do East African steel / building-materials websites offer?

### Takeaway
Mabati Rolling Mills (Safal, Kenya) is the regional benchmark. It has a dealer, service-centre and installer finder, a WhatsApp click-to-chat link, a separate online shop and a "Virtual Roof Visualizer / Design My Home" configurator. Industrial players such as Kamal Steel (TZ) and Cimerwa (RW) run brochure-style corporate sites: products, tenders, callback forms. None shows prices, calculators or mobile-money checkout. Visible price lists and steel quantity calculators appear to be a gap among the regional mills that were checked.

### Cited Findings
- **Mabati Rolling Mills (mabati.com, Kenya / Safal Group).** Navigation: Who We Are, Building Solutions, Coated Steel, Resources (homeowner and professional guides), FAQs, MRM-Shop, Where to buy, Services, Our Projects, Contact Us. — [mabati.com](https://mabati.com/)
  - Where-to-buy has three parts: "MRM Retail Centres", "MRM Authorized Distributors" (Chandaria, Ouru, Cecypo, Megha are named) and an "MRM Installer Finder", plus "Find an MRM Service Centre Near You". — [mabati.com](https://mabati.com/)
  - The where-to-buy page lists showrooms and service centres (Kitengela, Kiambu, Nairobi-Ngong, Mombasa, Nyeri, Nakuru, Migori, Machakos) with addresses, phone numbers and "View Map" links. The headline CTA is "Talk to us… call us on +254788202020". — [Mabati where to buy](https://mabati.com/where-to-buy-mabati-in-kenya/)
  - It has a WhatsApp (wa.me) link in the social icons and a tel: link. — [mabati.com](https://mabati.com/)
  - E-commerce: "MRM-Shop" and "BUY NOW" go to a separate subdomain, shop.mabati.com (cart and checkout details were not verified). — [mabati.com](https://mabati.com/)
  - Visualiser: "Virtual Roof Visualizer – TRY IT OUT" and "DESIGN MY HOME" link to a separate subdomain, mysafari.mabati.com. The heavy interactive tool sits off the homepage. — [mabati.com](https://mabati.com/)
  - Resources include specification and technical sheets for all products, a "Storage & Handling of Steel" guide, a Warranty page, "MRM Deals", a newsletter, a podcast and YouTube, a Google-reviews link and testimonials. — [mabati.com](https://mabati.com/)
  - **Not found:** calculators or estimators, price lists, a quote-request form, mobile money, a language switcher. — [mabati.com](https://mabati.com/)
- **Kamal Steel (kamalsteel.co.tz, Tanzania).** Product navigation ("Other Products" → "Structural Steel Products", "Ready to Build Structures"). It uses a callback form, "Want Us Call You Back? Drop your name & phone no…", instead of a quote form. TBS and TÜV certification logos are shown as images. No dealer locator, WhatsApp link, price list, calculator, shop, mobile money or Swahili version was found. — [kamalsteel.co.tz](https://kamalsteel.co.tz/)
- **Cimerwa (cimerwa.rw, Rwanda cement).** Navigation: Our Story, Products, Sustainability, Investor relations, Newsroom, CSR, Careers, **Tenders**, Contact Us, plus Facebook, X, Instagram and LinkedIn. The first 100k characters showed no distributor list, WhatsApp, prices, calculator, shop, mobile money, language switcher or S-Mark. The page is very heavy: about 332k characters of text output, and only the first 30% was read. — [cimerwa.rw](https://www.cimerwa.rw/)
- **Third-party WhatsApp construction commerce:** Cutstruct describes an AI marketplace where buyers order construction materials in WhatsApp with instant price checks. This is vendor marketing, and the countries it serves were not confirmed. — [Cutstruct Substack](https://cutstruct.substack.com/p/ai-powered-marketplace-delivering)
- **Rwanda's new competitor/context: A1 Iron & Steel Rwanda Ltd (Musanze).** It is in Kimonyi Sector, Musanze. Production started in April 2026 and the Prime Minister inaugurated it in September 2026. Planned capacity is about 250,000 t/yr. It uses the DRI route from locally sourced iron ore, then melting, billet casting and downstream products. Products named: rebar, nails, pipes and other construction products. About 1,000 direct jobs. MD Himanshu Tiwari: "We aspire to build a strong regional brand 'Made in Rwanda, for Africa'". — [KT Press, Sept 2026](https://ktpress.rw/2026/09/rwanda-opens-steel-plant-with-bigger-ambition-to-end-a-150m-import-habit/)
  - Search summaries of other coverage give a wider product list: TMT bars, round bars, wire rods, angles, C-channels, flat bars, HR strips, hollow sections, binding wire, BRC mesh, barbed wire and welding rods. They also give a planned investment of about $20M and say the plant could cut the import bill by about $100M. These were taken from search snippets and the articles were not fetched. — [New Times: five things to know](https://www.newtimes.co.rw/article/38925/news/rwanda/a1-iron-steel-five-things-to-know-about-rwandas-new-metal-factory); [New Times: could halve $200m import bill](https://www.newtimes.co.rw/article/38894/news/rwanda/new-steel-factory-could-halve-rwandas-200m-import-bill); [MINICOM](https://www.minicom.gov.rw/news-detail/rwanda-to-have-its-first-ever-integrated-steel-plant)
  - Rwanda imported more than $150M of steel products in 2025. — [KT Press](https://ktpress.rw/2026/09/rwanda-opens-steel-plant-with-bigger-ambition-to-end-a-150m-import-habit/). Sources disagree on the size of the import bill: New Times headline "$200m", KT Press ">$150m in 2025".
  - SteelRwa Industries is an older Rwandan producer of rebar made from recycled scrap (15 years in operation). — [New Times on SteelRwa](https://www.newtimes.co.rw/article/38359/news/featured/15-years-of-rwandan-steel-how-steelrwa-has-contributed-to-construction-sector-growth/amp)

### Inferences
- Across the region, the digital pattern is **"find a dealer + call/WhatsApp us"**, not self-serve checkout. Even the most advanced site (Mabati) sends transactions through phone, WhatsApp, a shop subdomain and physical service centres.
- Heavy interactive experiences (Mabati's visualiser) live on **separate subdomains**, which keeps the main site light. This is a good pattern for a steel site that wants 3D.
- In the sample checked, **nobody publishes indicative rebar prices or a bar-weight / quantity calculator** (kg/m by diameter, bars per tonne). That is a likely differentiator for a Rwandan mill. Rwanda-specific verification of competitors is still needed (see Gaps).
- Cimerwa's "Tenders" nav item suggests Rwandan industrials treat procurement and tender notices as core site content.

### Gaps
- **Devki Group / Steel Makers:** devkigroup.com redirected (302) to a domain-parking page (domains.atom.com). The correct current domain was not found.
- **Roofings Group (Uganda):** www.roofings.co.ug failed DNS lookup. The correct domain was not identified.
- **SteelRwa (steelrwa.com):** the page is JS-rendered and came back empty. An earlier fetch "summary" claiming price lists, calculators, mobile money and a Kinyarwanda/French/English switcher was **hallucinated by the fetch tool and must not be used**. SteelRwa's site features are unknown.
- Not checked: Bamburi Cement, Doshi Group, Lodhia (TZ), Imana Steel (RW), Cimerwa's full page, shop.mabati.com checkout and payment methods (M-Pesa?).

## 2. How prevalent is WhatsApp / mobile-first commerce? Mobile, data and language stats

### Takeaway
Rwanda has near-universal mobile *connections* but low internet *use*: 34.2% of people online, about 20-21% unique mobile-internet subscribers, about 34% of households with a smartphone. Cost of devices and data is the main barrier. Online buyers are therefore a relatively small, mostly urban and professional group on phones. Phone calls and WhatsApp remain the default B2B channel. No hard statistic on WhatsApp use for building-materials B2B in Rwanda was found.

### Cited Findings
- Rwanda had 13.0M cellular connections (88.7% of population) in late 2025, 5.01M internet users (34.2% penetration, Oct 2025) and 9.64M people offline (65.8%). — [DataReportal Digital 2026 Rwanda](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-rwanda)
- Rwanda had 1.35M social media user identities (9.2%) in Oct 2025. Facebook ad reach was 1.35M, Instagram 576k, **LinkedIn 570k members (+26.7% YoY)** and X 302k. DataReportal does not report WhatsApp audience figures for Rwanda. — [DataReportal Digital 2026 Rwanda](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-rwanda)
- Jan 2025: 4.93M internet users and 13.3M mobile connections. — [DataReportal Digital 2025 Rwanda](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-rwanda)
- About 21% of Rwanda's population were unique mobile internet subscribers in 2025 (GSMA). — [GSMA newsroom](https://www.gsma.com/newsroom/?p=26120)
- About 20% of Rwandans use mobile internet and 34% of households own a smartphone. Cost is the main obstacle, especially smartphones in rural areas. — [TechCabal, Jun 2025](https://techcabal.com/2025/06/12/rwanda-internet-penetration-rate/); [AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202506170578.html)
- Africa-wide: about 63% of people live under mobile broadband coverage but don't use mobile internet (usage gap), against a 9% coverage gap. Affordability is the single biggest barrier. — [GSMA Mobile Economy Africa 2026](https://www.gsma.com/solutions-and-impact/connectivity-for-good/mobile-economy/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/The-Mobile-Economy-Africa-2026.pdf) (via summary)
- Kenya is among the top five African countries by WhatsApp user base. Sukhiba (Kenya) raised $1.5M seed funding for B2B conversational commerce on WhatsApp. — [TechCrunch, Oct 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/25/sukhiba-eyes-africa-growth)
- Kenyan hardware and building-materials merchants send catalogues, PDF proposals and videos to buyers via WhatsApp (promotional directory listing, unverified). — [Africa-business directory](https://directory.africa-business.com/business/b0d336203a/Nafis-Glass----Hardware-Merchants)

### Inferences
- With only about a third of Rwandans online but nearly everyone on a mobile line, a steel site should treat **click-to-call and WhatsApp (wa.me with a prefilled message such as product, diameter, quantity, district)** as primary CTAs, not secondary ones.
- LinkedIn growth (570k members) points to a reachable professional audience of engineers, contractors and procurement officers in Rwanda.
- Rwanda's official languages are Kinyarwanda, English, French and Swahili. That is well known but was **not sourced in this session**. A Kinyarwanda layer for fundis and hardware dealers, plus French for the DRC/Burundi export markets, is a reasonable inference.

### Gaps
- No source found for the share of B2B construction orders made via WhatsApp in Rwanda, Kenya or Uganda.
- No Rwanda mobile vs desktop web-traffic split was found (DataReportal 2026 did not report it in the fetched text; StatCounter was not checked).
- No price-per-GB of mobile data for Rwanda was found. ITU (via WorldData) gives about US$3.20/month for a basic mobile plan in 2025, which is a basket price, not per-GB. — [WorldData](https://www.worlddata.info/africa/rwanda/telecommunication.php)
- No sourced language-use statistics.

## 3. Rwanda-specific context: Made in Rwanda, RSB S-Mark, Umucyo, Musanze, EAC exports

### Takeaway
The S-Mark (RSB product certification) is the key trust signal. It is recognised across the EAC and explicitly helps in government tenders. Rebar sold in Rwanda must meet a 500 MPa minimum grade. Public buyers buy through Umucyo, Rwanda's e-procurement system. RPPA monitors Made in Rwanda in public procurement, but no binding construction-materials quota was confirmed. A1's Musanze location and "Made in Rwanda, for Africa" positioning point toward exports to neighbouring markets, though no specific countries were named in the fetched article.

### Cited Findings
- RSB product certification lets certified goods carry the **S-Mark**. RSB says the mark is useful for exporters and **helps in government tenders**, with surveillance and re-certification on a two-year cycle. The S-Mark logo is registered with the EAC Secretariat and recognised across partner states. — [RSB Product Certification](https://www.rsb.gov.rw/certifications/product-certification) (via search summary)
- RSB's 2019 journal lists steel bars, cement and iron sheets among certified construction materials. — [RSB Standards Journal Issue 16 (Made in Rwanda focus)](https://www.rsb.gov.rw/fileadmin/user_upload/Rwanda_Standards_Journal/Rwanda_Standards_Journal_Issue_16-May_2019_Focus_on_Made_in_Rwanda.pdf) (via search summary)
- RSB has issued a compliance reminder to "Manufacturers and Importers of Iron Bars". The details are in an attached PDF (556KB) that was not read. — [RSB notice](https://www.rsb.gov.rw/updates/news-detail/reminder-to-all-manufacturers-and-importers-of-iron-bars)
- RICA banned steel bars below 500 MPa upper yield strength, reportedly to align with the harmonised EAC reinforcing-steel standard. The article is older, so its current status should be checked. — [New Times: regulator explains ban on steel bars](https://www.newtimes.co.rw/article/2899/news/business/regulator-explains-ban-on-steel-bars)
- **Umucyo** is Rwanda's web-based e-procurement system. Its modules include advertisement, e-bidding, evaluation, contract management, inspection and acceptance, framework agreements, and **catalogue and "shopping mall"** modules. Rwanda was the first African country with full e-procurement. — [RPPA e-procurement](https://www.rppa.gov.rw/e-procurement); [World Bank feature](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/feature/2018/09/05/how-rwanda-became-the-first-african-country-with-an-electronic-procurement-system)
- RPPA monitors and assesses "the implementation of Made in Rwanda in Public Procurement". Stakeholders have proposed registering more local products in Umucyo so officials can apply local preference, and setting a minimum share of local materials in public construction. These are proposals, not confirmed rules. — [RPPA: promotion of local products](https://www.rppa.gov.rw/1/promotion-of-local-products-for-sustainable-development-in-public-procurement); [RPPA services](https://www.rppa.gov.rw/services)
- Construction firms apply for contractor categorisation via Umucyo. — [RPPA FAQs](https://www.rppa.gov.rw/faqs)
- Open contracting data for Rwanda (RPPA) is published. — [Open Contracting 2024](https://www.open-contracting.org/2024/11/13/rwanda-a-new-open-contracting-portal-to-promote-effective-and-inclusive-procurement/)
- A1 plans to make products Rwanda now imports and later expand into "neighboring markets". — [KT Press](https://ktpress.rw/2026/09/rwanda-opens-steel-plant-with-bigger-ambition-to-end-a-150m-import-habit/)

### Inferences
- For government and developer buyers, the site should show **S-Mark certificate numbers or downloads, the grade (B500 / 500 MPa), mill test certificates, and Umucyo supplier registration**. RSB itself says the S-Mark helps win tenders.
- A "Tenders / Procurement" page (as Cimerwa has) and downloadable compliance documents (RDB registration, tax clearance, S-Mark) suit procurement officers who work on desktop within Umucyo.
- Musanze borders the DRC (Goma/Rubavu corridor) and Uganda. A French-language export page is a sensible inference, but no sourced trade-flow data was gathered.

### Gaps
- The exact standard numbers and steel-bar requirements in the RSB announcement PDF are unread.
- Whether A1 Iron & Steel holds an S-Mark yet was not found.
- No current legal margin of preference for Made in Rwanda in construction tenders was confirmed.
- Steel export volumes to the DRC, Uganda or Burundi, and EAC Common External Tariff treatment of steel, were not researched.

## 4. Performance constraints: speeds, page weight, implications for 3D/WebGL

### Takeaway
Rwanda has near-complete 4G coverage. Median fixed broadband is about 40 Mbps, and the last mobile figure found is about 21 Mbps (dated). The binding constraint is **data cost and low-end smartphones**, not raw bandwidth. A heavy WebGL homepage would cost users data and exclude much of the audience, so 3D should be opt-in or lazy-loaded (as Mabati does with a separate subdomain).

### Cited Findings
- Median fixed download speed in Rwanda was 40.38 Mbps (Ookla, to Aug 2025), up 32.4% YoY. — [DataReportal Digital 2026 Rwanda](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-rwanda)
- Median mobile download speed was about 21 Mbps per an early-2024 summary. That is dated, and no 2025 Ookla mobile figure was found. — via search summary, [DataReportal Digital 2026 Rwanda](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-rwanda) / [Ookla-based summaries](https://africa.com/a-number-of-african-countries-have-surged-in-global-rankings-that-gauge-internet-speeds) (low confidence)
- 4G coverage in Rwanda is estimated at 100% for 2025 by Statista, while WorldData gives 98.76% with no date. — [Statista](https://statista.com/outlook/co/digital-connectivity-indicators/rwanda); [WorldData](https://www.worlddata.info/africa/rwanda/telecommunication.php)
- Affordability of devices and data is the largest barrier to mobile internet use in Africa. — [GSMA Mobile Economy Africa 2026](https://www.gsma.com/solutions-and-impact/connectivity-for-good/mobile-economy/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/The-Mobile-Economy-Africa-2026.pdf); [TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2025/06/12/rwanda-internet-penetration-rate/)
- Mabati hosts its 3D roof visualiser on a separate subdomain (mysafari.mabati.com), launched from a CTA rather than loaded on the homepage. — [mabati.com](https://mabati.com/)

### Inferences
- Speeds of 20-40 Mbps can deliver a WebGL scene, but buyers pay per MB and many use entry-level Android phones. A multi-MB 3D homepage (three.js plus GLB models plus HDR textures) adds data cost and GPU and thermal load for little gain for a rebar buyer.
- Recommended pattern: a light static or poster-image hero (target under about 1 MB first load), with 3D as a click-to-load or separate route, plus `prefers-reduced-data` / Save-Data handling. Product data, the price/quantity calculator and WhatsApp CTAs must work without JS-heavy assets.
- Cimerwa's very large page payload (330k+ characters of text) is a cautionary example of regional corporate bloat.

### Gaps
- No verified 2025/2026 Ookla median **mobile** speed for Rwanda (check the Ookla Speedtest Global Index).
- No per-GB data price for Rwanda (check ITU/A4AI or RURA tariffs).
- No device-mix data (Android share, typical RAM) for Rwanda.
- No measured page weights (Lighthouse/WebPageTest) of the competitor sites.
