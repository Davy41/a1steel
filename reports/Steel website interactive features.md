# Trade spectacle for tools buyers actually use

The redesigned A1 Iron & Steel homepage is a strong brand showcase, but it is **not enough** on its own. It gives visitors one product-exploration toy (the WebGL bundle) and one way to convert (the quote form). The evidence says buyers come for four things the page lacks: a way to reach a person instantly (WhatsApp and click-to-call), proof of compliance (S-Mark, 500 MPa grade, mill test certificates, datasheets), a self-service sizing tool (a bar weight and quantity calculator), and an answer to "where do I buy and roughly what does it cost". Engineers rank datasheets (79%) and parametric product selection (66%) far above 3D graphics (30%) and chatbots (9%). The Indian TMT brands that lead in dealer-driven rebar markets, Tata and JSW, put calculators, guides, dealer locators and WhatsApp on or near the homepage. The East African mills checked, Mabati included, publish no prices and no steel calculator, so A1 can stand out cheaply in the region. The second problem is weight. In Rwanda only about 34% of people are online, data cost is the main barrier, and a three.js hero loaded at page start works against the Core Web Vitals that drive mobile conversion. So keep the 3D bundle, but load it after the page content as an opt-in layer behind a poster image. Then add a compact "tools band" (calculator, specs and certificates, where to buy, WhatsApp) to the homepage, and push the deeper tools onto subpages.

## Buyers want specs, prices and self-service more than spectacle

The largest recent survey of technical buyers, TREW Marketing and GlobalSpec's 2025 State of Marketing to Engineers (about 1,000 respondents), says buyers spend **60% of the buying process online before talking to anyone**. Supplier websites are their top source, used by **73%** ([TREW/GlobalSpec 2025](https://www.trewmarketing.com/hubfs/Technical%20Resources/Research/2025%20SMTE%20Research/PDF/2025%20State%20of%20Marketing%20to%20Engineers%20Research.pdf)). When asked which content is most valuable, **79% named datasheets**, 37% CAD drawings and 35% product demo videos. When asked which interactive tools are most valuable, **66% chose product selection or parametric search** and 59% comparison tools. Only **30% chose 2D/3D interactive graphics**, 22% quote generators and **9% chatbots** (same report). For engineers, "informative and well-maintained website" is the strongest credibility signal (4.0 of 5), ahead of customer references (3.8), awards (3.2) and social media (2.4). The most common trigger for contacting sales, after technical complexity, is pricing or stock questions (**40%**). Gartner found that **61% of B2B buyers prefer a rep-free experience** (67% in a 2026 follow-up), and that **69% see inconsistencies** between a seller's website and what its sales reps say ([Gartner, Jun 2025](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-sales-survey-finds-61-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-buying-experience); [Gartner, Mar 2026](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-03-09-gartner-sales-survey-finds-67-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-experience)). Nielsen Norman Group's B2B testing is older (2006), but it ranked **price as the single most-wanted piece of information**, well above availability. It also called gating content behind registration hostile to users ([NN/g, B2B Usability](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/b2b-usability/)).

Two cautions apply to A1. TREW's sample leans toward electronics and automation, and no buyer survey specific to steel or construction materials was found. Some of A1's buyers are also fundis, hardware dealers and homeowners, not engineers. The direction still holds: the current homepage gives no ungated specs, no price signal and no sizing tool. Its one interactive centerpiece is the category buyers rank near the bottom.

## Rivals split into homeowner toolkits and engineer document hubs

The global field divides by audience. Indian TMT brands sell through dealers to homeowners and masons, so they bring tools to the surface. Tata Steel's Aashiyana platform has a 5-step building-cost estimator on its homepage, links to a rebar estimator (slabs, beams, columns), fencing and shed estimators, a dealer and contractor directory, and floating WhatsApp, chat and "order on call" buttons ([Aashiyana homepage](https://aashiyana.tatasteel.com/in/en.html)). It reported **Rs 35.5 bn GMV in FY2024-25 (+60% YoY) and 110,000+ registered users** ([Construction World](https://www.constructionworld.in/amp/steel-news/tata-steel-targets-rs-70-billion-gmv-for-aashiyana-in-fy26/77534)). That figure measures the whole commerce platform, not the calculators alone. JSW Neosteel routes visitors through homepage cards for four audiences (Home Owner, Engineer, Contractor, Distributor). Each card leads to a steel calculator, a three-phase building guide, **8 downloadable sample structural drawings**, and a State-to-District distributor locator. The site also has "Know Current Prices", a brochure download, a floating WhatsApp button and a fraud alert ([JSW Neosteel](https://www.jswneosteel.in/); [Home Owner Zone](https://www.jswneosteel.in/solutions/home-owner-zone)). Kamdhenu fights counterfeits with a stamp on every metre, a red bundle strip and a **QR code on the bundle tag**, and tells buyers to check them when the steel is unloaded on site ([Kamdhenu Nxt](https://kamdhenulimited.com/product/kamdhenu-nxt)).

The US and global mills serve engineers and fabricators instead. Nucor runs a Document Hub (product guides, EPDs, SDSs, quality certifications) and links "Purchase steel" to a customer portal. No calculators were found on its site ([Nucor](https://www.nucor.com/)). Gerdau has a pop-up quote form, a public rolling schedule and certified mill test reports delivered through documents and its portal ([Gerdau](https://www2.gerdau.com/)). ArcelorMittal keeps section property tables and IFC/Revit BIM objects on separate microsites ([Constructalia BIM](https://constructalia.arcelormittal.com/en/tools/bim)). None of the eight named makers showed AR viewers, WebGL product viewers or virtual plant tours on their main sites. A1's 3D bundle is therefore unusual in the sector. That makes it a differentiator, but there is no proven model behind it.

East Africa is thinner. Mabati Rolling Mills, the regional benchmark, offers retail centres, authorized distributors, an installer finder, service centres with "View Map" links, a phone number as its headline call to action, WhatsApp, spec sheets for every product, a steel storage and handling guide, and a warranty page. Its heavy "Virtual Roof Visualizer" sits on a **separate subdomain** (mysafari.mabati.com), launched from a button ([mabati.com](https://mabati.com/); [where to buy](https://mabati.com/where-to-buy-mabati-in-kenya/)). Mabati, Kamal Steel and Cimerwa all lack **calculators and price lists**. Kamal relies on a callback form ([kamalsteel.co.tz](https://kamalsteel.co.tz/)). Cimerwa lists **Tenders** as a top navigation item ([cimerwa.rw](https://www.cimerwa.rw/)). SteelRwa, A1's closest domestic rebar rival, could not be read, so whether it offers these tools is unknown.

## Ranked recommendations: what goes where

The ranking weighs buyer-stated value (TREW, NN/g), proof that peers ship the feature (Tata, JSW, Mabati, Nucor, Gerdau), fit with Rwanda's phone-first, low-data audience, and build cost.

| Rank | Feature | Placement | Why (evidence) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **WhatsApp and click-to-call buttons**, with a prefilled `wa.me` message (product, size, quantity, district) | Homepage: sticky on mobile, plus in the hero | Regional pattern is "find a dealer + call/WhatsApp" ([Mabati](https://mabati.com/)); JSW floats WhatsApp ([JSW](https://www.jswneosteel.in/)); 88.7% of Rwandans have mobile connections but only 34.2% use the internet ([DataReportal 2026](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-rwanda)) |
| 2 | **Trust and compliance band**: S-Mark, 500 MPa grade, standards, sample mill test certificate, each linking to a PDF | Homepage, next to the capacity strip | RSB says the S-Mark helps win government tenders and is recognised across the EAC ([RSB](https://www.rsb.gov.rw/certifications/product-certification)); bars below 500 MPa are banned ([New Times](https://www.newtimes.co.rw/article/2899/news/business/regulator-explains-ban-on-steel-bars)); Nucor publishes certificates per mill ([Nucor certifications](https://Nucor.com/certifications)) |
| 3 | **Bar weight and quantity calculator** (kg/m by diameter, bars per tonne, slab/column estimate) | Compact 3-field version on the homepage; full tool on a subpage | Parametric tools top buyer rankings at 66% ([TREW](https://www.trewmarketing.com/hubfs/Technical%20Resources/Research/2025%20SMTE%20Research/PDF/2025%20State%20of%20Marketing%20to%20Engineers%20Research.pdf)); Tata and JSW both ship one; none found among the East African mills checked |
| 4 | **Ungated datasheets per product** (chemistry, mechanical properties, dimensions, tolerances, weight tables) | One click from each catalogue card; full set in a document hub subpage | Datasheets are the top-valued content at 79% (TREW); NN/g warns against gating ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/b2b-usability/)) |
| 5 | **Price signal**: an indicative range or "price as of [date]", or at least minimum order, lead time and stock status per product | In the existing catalogue picker, next to the price-request button | Price was the most-wanted B2B item ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/b2b-usability/)); NN/g prefers sample prices to complex calculators ([NN/g, Show Price](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/show-price/)); 40% contact sales over price or stock (TREW); JSW has "Know Current Prices" |
| 6 | **Audience entry cards**: Builder/homeowner, Engineer, Contractor/procurement, Dealer | Homepage, below the hero | JSW's four-zone pattern serves mixed audiences without clutter ([JSW](https://www.jswneosteel.in/)) |
| 7 | **Where to buy / dealer locator** with district filter and map links, plus "Become a dealer" | Subpage, linked from the homepage | Mabati and JSW both ship one; Rwanda's 30 districts suit a simple list better than a map app |
| 8 | **Procurement and tenders page**: S-Mark certificates, company registration, Umucyo supplier status, framework contracts | Subpage | Public buyers purchase through Umucyo ([RPPA](https://www.rppa.gov.rw/e-procurement)); Cimerwa lists Tenders in its navigation; RPPA monitors Made in Rwanda in procurement ([RPPA](https://www.rppa.gov.rw/1/promotion-of-local-products-for-sustainable-development-in-public-procurement)) |
| 9 | **Authenticity check**: how to spot genuine A1 bars (rolled marks, bundle tag, QR code) | Subpage, plus one homepage line | Kamdhenu's three-part check and site-delivery advice ([Kamdhenu](https://kamdhenulimited.com/product/kamdhenu-nxt)); JSW's fraud alert |
| 10 | **Guides and sample drawings**: storage and handling, a builder's rebar guide, standard drawings for foundation, column and slab | Subpage hub | JSW's 8 drawing PDFs; Mabati's storage guide; Tata's building guides |
| 11 | **Short "ore to bar" process story** (DRI, melt, billet, rolling) built on native scroll | Homepage section or About page | Zekelman's immersive piece had the lowest exit rate and 3x session time (agency-reported) ([MX Group](https://themxgroup.com/work/interactive-visualization-tool-shows-steel-products-everywhere)); NN/g allows scroll effects only when short, below the fold and not scrolljacked ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolljacking-101/)) |
| 12 | **Mill test certificate lookup by heat number** | Later, as a subpage or portal | None of the makers examined offers this publicly, so it could set A1 apart, but there is no proven precedent |

**What to skip.** Skip a chatbot: only 9% of buyers value one, and a WhatsApp button covers the same need. Skip full-page scrolljacking: NN/g found task-focused users disoriented, some leaving the page ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolljacking-101/)). Skip AR and a virtual plant tour on the homepage: no steel maker examined ships either, and the B2C uplift figures for AR come from Shopify's own marketing ([Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/ca/blog/3d-model-products)). Skip online checkout and mobile-money payment for now: even Mabati routes transactions through phone, service centres and a separate shop subdomain. Skip an Aashiyana-scale homeowner platform: Tata's results come from a whole commerce operation, not from widgets. Skip any live "sustainability tracker" unless it is backed by real EPD or CO2 data. Only 30% of engineers rate social responsibility as extremely or very important (TREW), so the 100,000-tree section should support the page, not lead it.

## Verdict on the current homepage: keep the craft, demote the 3D, add the plumbing

Taking each element in turn: the **13-product catalogue picker** is the page's strongest asset. It works as a lightweight version of the product-selection tool buyers rank first. It needs a datasheet link, weight per metre, sizes and standards, and a price, minimum order or lead-time signal on each card. The **quote form** is the right endpoint, but buyers do not value quote forms as research tools (22%), and much of Rwanda's audience would rather tap WhatsApp or a phone number than fill in fields. Kamal Steel's two-field callback form ([kamalsteel.co.tz](https://kamalsteel.co.tz/)) shows a lighter option worth offering next to the full form. The **capacity strip** listing six mills works as a trust signal. It becomes much stronger when it sits beside S-Mark and grade proof that links to real documents. The **board quote, tree section and media photos** are fine supporting content, but they currently take up space that the tools band should hold.

The **WebGL bundle** is the hard call. It is distinctive, and making a click open the product in the catalogue is the right pattern: Zekelman's case showed that immersive pieces pay off when they send users back to product pages. But Google's own model-viewer documentation warns that loading a 3D library and model at page start "will tend to destroy" Lighthouse scores ([modelviewer.dev](https://modelviewer.dev/examples/lighthouse.html)). Placing it beside the hero tagline makes it a likely Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) candidate. Keep it, but load it only after the page content: show a static poster render first, then start three.js when the browser is idle or when the visitor taps or hovers. Skip it entirely when the visitor has Save-Data or reduced motion turned on, or when the browser lacks WebGL. The **molten-steel margin line** passes NN/g's test only if it decorates normal scrolling and never takes it over. It must stop under `prefers-reduced-motion`, which is the sufficient technique for WCAG 2.3.3 ([W3C C39](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/css/C39.html)).

## Performance budget for Rwanda's phone-first, pay-per-megabyte buyers

Rwanda's constraint is not bandwidth. 4G coverage is put at about 100% ([Statista](https://statista.com/outlook/co/digital-connectivity-indicators/rwanda)), and median fixed download speed is **40.38 Mbps** ([DataReportal 2026](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-rwanda)). The mobile figure of about 21 Mbps is dated and low-confidence. The binding constraints are cost and devices: about **20-21% of Rwandans use mobile internet and 34% of households own a smartphone**, and cost is the main obstacle ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2025/06/12/rwanda-internet-penetration-rate/); [GSMA](https://www.gsma.com/newsroom/?p=26120)). Affordability is the largest barrier across Africa ([GSMA Mobile Economy Africa 2026](https://www.gsma.com/solutions-and-impact/connectivity-for-good/mobile-economy/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/The-Mobile-Economy-Africa-2026.pdf)). Many visitors pay for every megabyte on entry-level Android phones. Speed pays off commercially: in web.dev case studies, Lazada's 3x faster LCP brought **+16.9% mobile conversion**, and the agriculture marketplace Agrofy's 70% LCP improvement cut **load abandonment by 76%** ([web.dev](https://web.dev/case-studies/vitals-business-impact)).

Work to Google's "good" thresholds at the 75th percentile: **LCP of 2.5 s or less, INP of 200 ms or less, CLS of 0.1 or less** ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/defining-core-web-vitals-thresholds)). In practice, the first view should weigh roughly **1 MB or less**, with the LCP element being the headline text or a compressed poster image, never the canvas. Load three.js and the model files lazily, as Google recommends for the library itself, not only the models. Use Draco compression only when it saves more than its decoder costs (over 100 KB) ([modelviewer.dev](https://modelviewer.dev/examples/lighthouse2.html)). The catalogue, calculator, datasheet links, WhatsApp and the quote form must all work without the 3D code. Serve product photos as responsive AVIF/WebP with set width and height so the layout does not shift. Make the four media photos and the tree section lazy-load. Mabati's approach of putting heavy interactive tools on a separate route or subdomain is the regional precedent. Cimerwa's very large homepage payload is the regional warning. Two numbers are still missing and should be measured before launch: a current Ookla mobile median for Rwanda, and the actual page weight of the current build in WebPageTest on a throttled mid-range Android profile.

## Conclusion

A1's redesign is built for a jury, while its buyers are a foreman on a phone in Musanze, a procurement officer checking Umucyo, and an engineer who wants a datasheet. The opportunity is unusually cheap. In the East African sites checked, nobody publishes a steel calculator or a price signal. The most trusted certification in the market (S-Mark) is one A1 can surface with a single PDF link, if it holds or obtains one; this research could not confirm A1's S-Mark status. WhatsApp already serves as the region's working B2B channel. These are low-tech features, and they are the ones that win in this market.

Keep the 3D bundle, but treat it as the frosting: a loaded-on-demand layer that sends people into the catalogue. The real work is the plumbing around it: specs that match what sales quotes (Gartner's 69% inconsistency finding is a warning), a tools band, and a fast first view. One untested idea deserves a pilot. No mill examined offers a public mill-test-certificate lookup by heat number. A new Made in Rwanda plant trying to win trust against imports could make that its signature proof feature, more useful than any animation.
